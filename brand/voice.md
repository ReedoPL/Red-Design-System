# Red voice: proposed working guidance

The supplied PDF does not include a full tone-of-voice guide. These are proposed copy defaults for consistent internal design, based on the warm, family-oriented character of the artwork. Approved Red copy supplied for a campaign takes precedence.

## Sound like a helpful person

Warm, clear, calm and practical. Write to one person. Respect that people may be exploring rather than ready to buy. Use British English, sentence case and familiar verbs. Keep promises precise.

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| “A little clarity about life insurance.” | “The ultimate revolutionary protection solution.” |
| “Take time to understand the options.” | “Act now before it’s too late.” |
| “Think about the people who depend on you.” | “What will your family do when you’re gone?” |
| “The premium is the amount you pay for your policy.” | Unexplained insurance jargon |
| “Explore a quote” when the destination is a quote journey | “Get covered” before underwriting or acceptance |

## Names and roles

Use **Red** in prose and **Red.co.uk** for the domain. The supplied artwork spells the wordmark with a full stop; preserve its file instead of reproducing it with a font. Write **Protect Line** as two words when it legitimately appears in a supplied approved statement. Do not transfer Protect Line’s purpose or voice guide to Red as if it were a Red source.

Where the service is non-advised, describe help with understanding options and the customer’s choice. Do not call copy or illustrated characters evidence of a regulated advice service. “Agent” or “team” is a safer working label than an invented “financial adviser” title unless the actual service has been verified.

## Evidence discipline

Do not invent customer quotations, ratings, insurer panels, savings, prices, eligibility, contact details, offer values, call-back times or payout guarantees. Do not imply automatic acceptance, immediate cover or a universal product benefit. Use `[REQUIRES SOURCE: specific item]` when necessary.

Explain limitations alongside benefits in the consuming design using the approved product wording. Template explanations are illustrative and must be reviewed for the actual product and audience. A mascot’s relaxed expression is not a guarantee of financial peace of mind.

## Structure

One meaningful headline; one short paragraph that adds information; a next action whose label matches its destination. Avoid repetitive benefit claims. Long explanations belong in open, left-aligned sections or FAQs, not tiny disclaimers.
